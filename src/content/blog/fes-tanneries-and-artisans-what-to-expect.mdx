The Chouara tannery is the image most people have of Fes. It is worth seeing,
but it is a small part of a much bigger craft economy.

## The tanneries

Hides are cured, soaked and dyed in open stone vats, a process that has changed
little over centuries. You view it from the terraces of nearby leather shops,
where staff often hand out sprigs of mint to offset the smell. Go in the morning,
when work is underway.

## Beyond the tanneries

The medina also has working weavers, brass and copper beaters, woodworkers and
potters. Many of them are still organised by quarter, and a guide can introduce
you to makers rather than shops.

- **Weaving.** Hand looms producing cloth and cushion covers.
- **Metalwork.** Copper and brass hammered in small workshops near the Seffarine
  square.
- **Pottery.** Fes blue ceramics, often finished in studios just outside the
  walls.

## Buying, if you want to

There is no pressure to buy. If you do, ask about materials and how long a piece
took to make, and expect to bargain politely. Our
[Fes Medina & Artisans tour](/tours/fes-medina-and-artisans) includes workshop
visits with no shopping obligation.
