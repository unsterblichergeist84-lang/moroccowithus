If your trip includes both cities - and most do - the order changes the
experience more than people expect.

## How they differ

**Fes** is older, denser and less used to tourism.
[Fes el Bali](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/170) - a UNESCO World Heritage site -
has around nine thousand lanes, no cars, and a working economy of tanners,
weavers and metalworkers. It can feel overwhelming on day one and rewarding by
day three.

**Marrakech** is louder and faster but also more legible. The souks are busier
with visitors, the riads are more polished, and there is more of a scene -
gardens, rooftops, day trips into the Atlas.

## Which first

We usually suggest **Fes first**. It asks more of you, and it is easier to arrive
with energy than to face it at the end of a long trip. Marrakech afterwards feels
comparatively easy, and it connects well to the desert or the coast for the
second half.

If you only have time for one, choose by temperament: Fes for depth and craft,
Marrakech for energy and the mountains behind it.

## Getting between them

The [train](https://www.oncf-voyages.ma/) takes about seven hours and is
comfortable. Driving is a similar time but lets you stop at
[Volubilis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/836) and
[Meknes](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/793), or in Ifrane and the cedar forest
on the way. Flying is possible but rarely worth the airport time. See
[driving distances](/blog/driving-distances-in-morocco) if you are weighing the
drive.
