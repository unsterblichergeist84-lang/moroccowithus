People ask for the best time to come and expect one answer. Morocco is big
enough that the honest reply is "it depends where you are going". Here is the
rough shape of the year.

## The short version

| Season | Cities | Sahara | High Atlas | Coast |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spring (Mar-May) | excellent | excellent | good, some snow early | breezy, cool |
| Summer (Jun-Aug) | hot | very hot | pleasant | best |
| Autumn (Sep-Nov) | excellent | excellent | excellent | warm, calm |
| Winter (Dec-Feb) | mild days, cold nights | cold nights, clear light | snow, some passes close | quiet |

## Spring and autumn

March to May and October to November are the two windows that work almost
everywhere at once. Comfortable in the medinas, warm but not punishing in the
desert, and the mountain roads are open. These are also the busy months, so the
best riads and desert camps book up two to three months out - see
[how far ahead to get in touch](/blog/what-private-tour-means).

## Summer

Marrakech and the southern valleys get very hot - well over 40C is normal in
July and August. The desert is for the hardy. The Atlantic coast, though, is at
its best: Essaouira and the beaches north of it stay cool and bright while the
interior bakes.

## Winter

Underrated. The cities are mild by day and the light in the desert is
extraordinary, though nights on the dunes drop close to freezing and you will
want the extra blanket. Snow can briefly close the
[Tizi n'Tichka pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tizi_n%27Tichka) between
Marrakech and Ouarzazate; we watch the forecast and reroute if needed.
