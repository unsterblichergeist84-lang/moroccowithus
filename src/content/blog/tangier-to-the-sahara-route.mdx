Travelling from the north of Morocco to the desert is a long way, and the right
rhythm matters. Here is how our nine-day route breaks it up.

## The shape of the trip

- **Tangier to Chefchaouen.** A short first leg, ending in the blue medina.
- **Chefchaouen to Fes.** A half-day drive and a full day in the old city.
- **Fes to Merzouga.** Two days over the Middle Atlas and down the Ziz valley.
- **The dunes.** Two nights in a desert camp so there is time to enjoy it.
- **Back to Marrakech.** The Todgha Gorge, Ait Benhaddou and the Atlas crossing.

## The long days

Fes to Merzouga is the longest stretch, so it is split with a night in Midelt.
The final day, from the gorges to Marrakech, is also long. Start early and plan
stops. See [driving distances in Morocco](/blog/driving-distances-in-morocco)
for typical times.

## One-way or loop

The route is one-way, ending in Marrakech, which avoids repeating ground.
A flight home from Marrakech works well.
