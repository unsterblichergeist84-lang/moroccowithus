The desert night is the part of the trip people remember most, and also the part
they know least about beforehand. Here is roughly how it goes.

## Getting there

You arrive at the edge of
[Erg Chebbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_Chebbi) in the late afternoon and
transfer the last stretch by 4x4, or by camel if you would rather. The camp sits
in a hollow between dunes, out of sight of the road and the village.

## The camp itself

A permanent camp is not a resort and not a bivouac. Expect a proper bed, rugs, a
washstand or a simple private bathroom, and a shared tent for dinner. Solar
lighting, no mains power. Phone signal is patchy, which most people end up
grateful for.

## The evening

Dinner is usually a tagine cooked on site, eaten late. Afterwards there is often
a fire and drums, and then it gets very quiet and very dark. On a clear moonless
night the stars are genuinely startling.

## The morning

Worth waking for. The light comes up slowly and the dunes change colour by the
minute. A short camel walk to the top of a nearby crest for sunrise is the
standard move, and it earns its early start.

## What to bring

A warm layer - desert nights are cold even in spring - a head torch, and shoes
you do not mind emptying sand out of. The [packing guide](/blog/packing-for-morocco)
has the full list, and [when to visit](/blog/when-to-visit-morocco) covers how
cold the nights actually get.

The [Fes to the Sahara](/tours/fes-sahara-crossing) and
[Marrakech loop](/tours/marrakech-sahara-loop) itineraries both include two
nights at camp rather than one.
