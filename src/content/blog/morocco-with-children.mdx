Morocco is a good country to travel in with kids. People are warm towards
children, the distances are manageable if you plan around them, and there is
plenty that lands well with younger travellers.

## What tends to work

- **The desert.** Camels, dunes to run down,
  [a night in a tent](/blog/a-night-in-the-sahara), stars. This is usually the
  highlight for children and adults alike.
- **[Ait Benhaddou](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/444) and the kasbahs.** They
  read as castles, which helps.
- **Essaouira.** Space to move, a beach, the harbour, fewer crowds than
  Marrakech.
- **Cooking a tagine** at a riad - a hands-on hour that breaks up a city day.

## Where to ease off

- **Long driving days.** Split them. A six-hour leg is hard on everyone; two
  three-hour days with a stop are fine. The
  [distance table](/blog/driving-distances-in-morocco) shows which legs to
  worry about.
- **Dense medina mornings.** Shorter guided walks, earlier starts, an ice cream
  built in.
- **Peak summer.** The heat in Marrakech and the south is genuinely tough for
  small children. Spring, autumn or a coast-weighted trip is kinder.

## Practicalities

Car seats can be arranged with notice. Riads often have family rooms or
connecting rooms. Pharmacies are good and widespread. Tap water is not for
drinking - bottled only.
