People arrive braced for a week of the same dish. The reality is more varied
than that, though tagine does earn its reputation.

## Breakfast

Bread, olive oil, honey, jam, a hard-boiled egg, mint tea or coffee. In the
riads there is often msemen (a flaky griddle bread) or beghrir (a spongy
pancake) too. Simple and the same most mornings, and better than it sounds.

## Lunch

The bigger meal for many Moroccans. On driving days it might be a roadside grill
- brochettes, salad, bread - or a tagine at a place your driver rates. In the
cities, a long lunch on a rooftop is one of the pleasures of the trip.

## Dinner

Lighter, later. Harira soup, a tagine or a couscous (traditionally Friday),
grilled fish on the coast, and fruit to finish. At the desert camp it is
usually a vegetable and meat tagine cooked on site.

## Worth seeking out

- **[Essaouira](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/753)** for fish straight off the
  boat, grilled at the port.
- **Fes** for the old sweet-savoury dishes - pastilla, lamb with prunes.
- **Marrakech** for the
  [Jemaa el-Fnaa](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/331) food stalls, chaotic and
  fun, plus the quieter modern places in Gueliz.

## Dietary needs

Vegetarian is easy - the vegetable tagine and couscous are everywhere. Vegan and
gluten-free take a little more arranging but are manageable; tell us in advance
and we brief the riads and camps. This is part of what
[a private trip](/blog/what-private-tour-means) makes straightforward.
